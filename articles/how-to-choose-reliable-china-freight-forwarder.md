An importer does not need the freight forwarder with the biggest advertisement. The importer needs a provider that can understand the cargo, define the service scope, identify foreseeable risks and remain accountable when the shipment changes.

Price matters, but a freight quotation is only useful when the information and included services are comparable.

![China logistics team reviewing an export shipment](/images/about-tengyoda-team.webp)

## 1. Confirm the company identity

Ask for the company's registered name, business registration information, operating address and official contact details. The beneficiary name on a commercial invoice should be checked against the contracting party or an explained payment arrangement.

For ocean freight, ask whether the provider holds or operates under the relevant NVOCC arrangements. If a qualification is important to your due diligence, request the current document rather than relying only on a logo or website statement.

Unexpected changes to bank details should always be verified through a previously confirmed communication channel.

## 2. Look for experience relevant to your cargo and route

A company can be experienced in one type of logistics but unfamiliar with another. Ask about shipments with similar characteristics:

- Origin city and Chinese loading port
- Destination country and port
- FCL, LCL, air freight or express
- Furniture, building materials, machinery or consumer products
- Heavy, oversized, fragile or restricted cargo
- Multi-supplier consolidation
- Customs and biosecurity requirements at destination

Experience should be demonstrated through a clear explanation of the process and risks, without disclosing another customer's confidential documents.

## 3. Test the questions the forwarder asks

A reliable forwarder should request more than “How many cubic metres?” For an initial review, expect questions about:

- Product name, material and intended use
- Number and type of packages
- Packed dimensions and gross weight
- Pickup city and loading conditions
- Cargo-ready date
- Destination port or delivery postcode
- Incoterm and required service scope
- Batteries, liquids, powders, magnets, chemicals or timber
- Stackability, fragility and unloading requirements

If a final price is offered before essential cargo details are known, ask what assumptions were used.

## 4. Compare quotation scope, not only the headline rate

Two quotations may use the same route but include different services. Request a written breakdown covering:

1. Factory pickup or supplier delivery
2. China warehouse and handling
3. Export customs declaration and documentation
4. Main freight and carrier surcharges
5. Destination shipping-line and terminal charges
6. Customs clearance, duty and tax responsibility
7. Truck delivery, unloading and equipment
8. Inspection, storage, demurrage and detention exclusions

The cheapest port-to-port rate can produce a higher landed cost if destination charges are unclear.

## 5. Check how cargo changes are handled

Final dimensions can differ from supplier estimates. Production may finish late. A vessel can roll cargo or change its schedule. Ask how the forwarder updates the quotation, communicates deadlines and documents the customer's approval when circumstances change.

Professional communication includes explaining what is known, what is estimated and what remains outside the forwarder's control.

## 6. Review warehouse and consolidation capability

If you buy from several suppliers, ask how the warehouse identifies incoming orders. The process should use an importer reference, purchase-order number or shipping mark.

Clarify which services are included:

- Package count and arrival photographs
- External dimension and weight measurement
- Visible-damage reporting
- Relabelling or shipping marks
- Palletising or export packing
- Detailed product inspection

Basic receiving is not the same as quality control. A detailed inspection must have an agreed checklist.

## 7. Evaluate destination knowledge without expecting guarantees

A China forwarder should help prepare accurate origin documents and coordinate the destination handoff. However, import eligibility, duty, tax and government inspections depend on the cargo and destination authorities.

Be cautious if anyone guarantees customs clearance without reviewing the product, material, value and importer status.

## 8. Start with a controlled shipment

For a new relationship, a smaller or lower-risk shipment can help both parties test communication, documentation and payment procedures. Record who is responsible for each stage and keep final approvals in writing.

## Questions to ask before appointing a China freight forwarder

- What is your registered company name and operating address?
- Which cargo types and trade lanes do you handle regularly?
- What information is missing from my enquiry?
- Which charges are included and excluded?
- Who handles destination clearance and delivery?
- How do you identify goods from multiple suppliers?
- What happens if dimensions, weight or schedule changes?
- Which documents will I receive before departure?
- Who is my daily shipment contact?
